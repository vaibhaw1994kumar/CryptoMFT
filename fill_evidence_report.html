Is the curve smooth around 1.0, and if not, why?

  • It is not smooth. Hit rate: 59.8% (n = 0.95), 80.5% (1.00), 71.4% (1.05). Premature fills jump from 6.6% to 26.0% below 1 (and 30.5% at 0.90), late fills from 11.7% to 20.5% above 1.
  • Special-cased path for n = 1? Yes, _consume_proportional takes frac = min(1, ahead / level) when n is exactly 1.0 and ahead**n / (ahead**n + behind**n) otherwise. The two are the same function: test_share_ahead_is_continuous_in_n_at_one compares them on 400 random (ahead, level, decrease) cases including ahead > level and finds agreement to 12 places at n = 1.0 and to within 1% at n = 0.999 and 1.001. The branch is therefore not the cause.
  • behind = level_before − ahead: level_before is the displayed level from the feed, which holds other participants' orders only, so it does not include our own order. It can be zero or negative when the simulated queue ahead is as large as or larger than the displayed level (queue-stress multiplier or a stale feed); the code clips it at 0, which makes the share 1 for every n, exactly as the proportional formula does (test_edge_cases_nothing_ahead_and_nothing_behind). ahead = 0 gives a share of 0 before any division, so there is no 0/0.
  • The real cause is the threshold that declares the queue empty. An order can only be filled by a depth decrease once queue_ahead ≤ 1e-12. With a constant share per cancel the queue decays geometrically and takes about 600 equal cancels of 5% to cross that threshold at n = 1, about 280 at n = 0.95 (half) and about 1,560 at n = 1.05 (2.6 times as many) (test_cancels_alone_empty_the_queue_ever_slower_as_n_rises). Below 1 many orders reach 'empty' through cancels alone and are then filled by the next unexplained decrease (premature); above 1 they do not and wait for a trade (late). When the queue ahead is small next to the volume behind it, the share is about (ahead/behind)n, so even a 5% change in n multiplies it several-fold (test_small_queue_ahead_is_very_sensitive_to_n).
  • So n = 1 is the point where cancels almost never empty the queue by themselves and trades decide: it works because it switches a fill path off, not because the data proved a share of exactly ahead/level.

6. Fill-evidence rule

What was built

MakerConfig.fill_evidence in {"off" (default), "trade_confirm", "fractional", "trade_confirm+fractional"} with evidence_window_ms (default 100) and evidence_fill_fraction (default 0.15). With "off" the engine is bit-identical to before (golden digests on six random-stream configurations in tests/test_fill_evidence.py; real replays in section 1d).

  • trade_confirm: an unexplained depth decrease or snapshot shrink that finds the queue ahead already at 0 does not fill. It records 'at front' (front_ts, front_decrease). A public trade at the order's price on the matching side, stamped within evidence_window_ms after the decrease, fills the order up to the trade's own size (times trade_fill_share) with cause trade_after_front, even though the trade/depth de-duplication would otherwise net that trade against the decrease. A trade through our price still follows the ordinary rule (fill_through_prints).
  • fractional: the fill from such a decrease is capped at evidence_fill_fraction × the decrease, and at the remaining size.
  • trade_confirm+fractional: trade_confirm, and the confirming fill is capped at fraction × the decrease.
  • Vanished level (delta_vanish): under every mode except off the order moves to the front (queue ahead set to 0) with no fill; only a trade fills it.
Interpretation to confirm (Decisions). The brief says 'no fill on a trade after the window'. I built it so that a trade after the window is not a confirmation (no bypass of the de-duplication, cause stays trade), but an order at the front is still filled by an ordinary later trade, because otherwise it could never fill again. A trade 30 ms after a decrease with a 10 ms window is netted by the 50 ms de-duplication and fills nothing, which is what the test asserts; a trade seconds later fills as an ordinary trade. Windows of 20 ms and above give identical hit, premature and late rates on these data (the calibration table shows only the tag of 18 to 36 fills changing from trade to trade_after_front as the window grows), because the de-duplication window is 50 ms.

Why the evidence rules lose hit rate on fresh-book orders

On the calibration orders, the proportional model's first fills come from trades (390), level-vanish (98), snapshot shrinks (76) and depth-delta shares (9). Of these, level-vanish and snapshot fills are mostly right on a fresh book (see the cause table in section 5 and below); the delta-share path, the one the brief worried about, accounts for only a handful of first fills. The rule gates all three paths, so it moves many correct fills to the next trade (late) while removing few premature ones. The same is visible with existing knobs: delta_fill_share = 0 also switches the level-vanish fill off, and costs 11 points of hit rate.

7. P&L-based weight for premature fills

Proxy. Per order and path (real | simulated): value = qty × price × markout(H)/10⁴ + qty × price × (−fee rate), in quote currency, where markout(H) is the signed move of the public trade tape H seconds after that path's first fill (+ favours the maker), qty is the real filled quantity for the real path and min(simulated fill, order size) for the simulated path, and the fee term is the order's own average maker rate from the execution records (a rebate on this account). Horizons +5 s and +30 s. Error cost = simulated value − real value for premature and late orders; for an unfilled simulated order whose real order filled, the missed opportunity = −(real value). Primary cost = the mean of the +5 s and +30 s costs in quote currency, fixed before looking at W. W = mean |premature cost| / mean |late cost|, calibration days, seen coins, proportional model (the reference), cluster-bootstrap 95% interval over coin-days (2,000 resamples).

W = 1.08 (95% interval 0.80 to 1.43) on 38 premature and 68 late orders from 14 coin-days. The weight of 3 used in the earlier asymmetric loss is outside this interval: in these data a premature fill is only slightly more costly than a late one.

3. Test #3 semantics (a level that vanishes)

Under every evidence mode a vanished level moves the order to the front with no fill, as the brief specified. On fresh-book orders, level-vanish first fills are right most of the time (calibration, seen coins: 98 such first fills, 88% within ±1 s, 1% premature), so withholding the fill costs hit rate (fractional mode, which differs from off essentially by this rule, loses 9.1% of hit on calibration). Decision: keep the specified semantics only inside fill_evidence (as built), or change the vanish rule to 'front, and fill the leftover only if a trade followed within the window' (not built; it would sit between the two).
