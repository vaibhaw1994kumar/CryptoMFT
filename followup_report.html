2. Strategy-level table redone (cash P&L, common-time equity, two-venue window)

What was wrong with the ending-equity table. The two venues' feeds in the data end at different times (feed table below). ending_equity values the final inventory with each venue's last mid, i.e. one venue's mid at the end of its feed and the other's hours earlier or later. A hedged pair (long on one venue, short on the other, net zero) then carries the price move between the two feed ends as profit or loss that no trade made. cash_pnl is not affected, and neither is the equity marked at a common time. The engine's own common_timestamp_equity could not repair this: it keeps only the last 32 mids per venue, so when the feeds end hours apart it has no mid at the common time and falls back to ending_equity (its staleness field reports inf in every run below). I therefore recorded a 1-second sample of each venue's mid during the runs and computed the common-time marks from it.

3. Depth-size question: record format, book reconstruction, verdict

Verdict. The ON reading (a cancel removes the level, its volume being the size that was removed) reconstructs the recorded snapshots far better than the legacy reading: top-5 match 91.3% [90.2, 92.4] against 68.8% [64.9, 72.6] on fresh-book periods (cluster-bootstrap 95% interval over 119 coin-days); the difference is 22.5% [19.2, 26.0]. Every one of the 17,611,768 cancel records in these coin-days carries a volume equal (within one lot) to the level size just before it, so a cancel is a full removal and the legacy reading re-creates every cancelled level at its old size. The fixed reading is correct.

The exchange's documented semantics

Bybit V5 public orderbook (spot, depth 50, push frequency 20 ms), as returned by the documentation page when fetched during this study: "If you receive an amount that is 0, delete the entry"; "If the entry exists, you simply update the value"; "The delta data has size=0, which means that all quotations for this price have been filled or cancelled." So the exchange's delta carries the level's new absolute size, and size 0 deletes the level. The collector turns a size-0 delta into a cancel record whose volume is the size that was removed (all cancel records here equal the level size before), and size changes into modify/add records with the new absolute size. That is exactly the ON reading. (The documentation page says nothing explicit on absolute-vs-change; the rule about updating the value implies absolute. The data settle it: modify records alternate up and down around the level size, never accumulate.)

Why vanish_clears_queue: the evidence from section 3

Section 3 settles that the depth-size fix is right, yet on the calibration days the legacy reading had scored a higher hit rate (84.7% against 80.5%, proportional, evidence off). To find what makes the correct reading slower than reality I traced the engine around orders that were late with the fix and on time without it (calibration days, seen coins; 31 late→hit and 10 hit→late orders). In the traced LTCUSDT order the real order filled at +9.30 s; the level (15.46 LTC) vanished at +9.31 s. With the fix, the vanish was processed as a decrease of the level; the trace shows the trade that filled the real order (1.0 LTC, printed 0.18 s later) changing nothing, which is what the trade/depth de-duplication does when it has already credited the trade to the decrease. The proportional rule had charged only the ahead share of the decrease to the queue, and 0.05 LTC of queue ahead was left (the order was 0.1) (queue state taken from the trace); that blocks any fill until a later trade, and the simulated order filled 48 s late. (I inferred the de-duplication step from the trace and the code; I did not step through it separately.) Without the fix the cancel was invisible as a decrease and the next snapshot (which shows the level gone) emptied the queue through the snapshot path, which charges the whole shrink to the queue ahead, by accident right. A level that vanishes cannot leave a queue ahead of an order that is still in the book, so the rule is: on a vanish, the queue ahead is zero. That is vanish_clears_queue.

Verdict on the sharp optimum. It does not disappear. With the current threshold the loss goes 0.211 at n = 1, 0.300 at 1.05 and 0.467 at 0.95. The one-lot and 5%-of-order thresholds and the vanish rule change the level of the curve but not its shape: with the vanish rule it is 0.128 at n = 1, 0.149 at 1.05, 0.162 at 1.1 (a gentle slope on the slow side, late fills rising from 4.1% to 8.4%) but 0.394 at 0.95. The cliff is on the n < 1 side and its cause is visible in the first-fill causes: with the vanish rule at n = 1 only 8 first fills are delta_share, at n = 0.95 there are 219 (124 of them premature). Each cancel moves the queue ahead by a constant share, so the queue shrinks geometrically; for n < 1 that share is larger, the queue reaches an empty state within many orders' lifetimes, and from then on every unexplained decrease fills the order. For n = 1 and above the queue rarely gets there. The optimum at n = 1 is therefore the edge of that behaviour, not a calibrated value, and the thresholds do not remove the edge (they only move where the queue counts as empty). Held-out days were not run for any other n, because none beats n = 1 on calibration.
