### C2 in detail: the Bybit crossed-book measurement

Method (read-only; two throwaway scripts in the session scratchpad, no write to `data/`): replay the Bybit book snapshots and per-level delta records of March 2024 through `new_backtest.OrderBook` (hybrid semantics, tape repair off) **with the crossed repair disabled**, and group delta records into messages by identical `exchange_ts` and `local_ts`. 20-minute slices from 00:00 UTC on three days (SELECT and TRAIN days), about 0.8-1.1 million deltas each, plus one run of the real repairing book to reproduce the backtester's own counter.

| Slice (20 min) | Deltas | Messages | BT `crossed_events` (repairing book) | Messages with a cross inside, gone by message end | Messages still crossed at their end | Deltas leaving the book crossed (no repair) | Deltas whose touch differs from the end-of-message touch (BT book) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024-03-05 00:00 | 1,073,057 | 11,978 | 1,258 | 1,246 | **0** | 163,730 | 232,753 (21.7 %) |
| 2024-03-08 00:00 | 774,488 | 11,966 | 975 | 973 | **0** | 111,679 | 138,771 (17.9 %) |
| 2024-03-12 00:00 | 773,879 | 11,958 | 885 | 879 | **0** | 93,382 | 154,971 (20.0 %) |

Reading:
- The backtester's counter is about 1 per second (1,258 in 1,198 s), as you said, and the transient count (1,246 messages) matches it.
- **No message ends crossed. Every cross is transient inside one exchange message**, a multi-level update applied one level at a time. Across all 35,902 messages the book after the repairing replay equals the book after the no-repair replay at every message end (touch and top 10 levels identical).
- The repair is therefore not wrong at message ends, but the backtester evaluates signals after **every single delta whose touch changed** (`backtester.py:2735`), so the strategy and the maker queue see half-applied messages: the post-delta touch differs from the end-of-message touch in about a fifth of all deltas (18-22 %). Crossing is only the visible symptom.
- Consequence for DAT05: the fix is "apply a message as one unit, validate at its end" (as the core's `MarketEvent` and the BT's own `queue_v2/rebuild.py` already do), not "invalidate the book on a cross", which would invalidate 12-15 % of deltas (the no-repair column above).
- Limits: 20 minutes per day from midnight UTC only, Bybit only (Binance March is best bid/ask only), message grouping assumes identical `exchange_ts` and `local_ts` is one exchange message (not proven from the exchange protocol), and I did not measure what the half-applied touch does to signals or fills.

## 4. Proposals (for Gate 1)

Aligned with `single_platform_plan.md` section 5: step 3 is an accounting shadow, step 4 is the deferred hedge child, step 5 the adapter. They are separate proposals with separate gates.

### 4A. Accounting-only shadow (plan step 3)

**What it is.** The unchanged backtester feeds its real fills, and nothing else, into the core's **ledger, fees, funding, margin and valuation**, and the shadow compares the results. The core **OMS and risk engine are not in the loop**: `oms.execute()` builds two fixed children at signal time and cannot follow a maker quote that may never fill, so it would invent orders and fills the backtester never had. The order lifecycle stays covered by the existing parity tests (`tests/test_order_state_parity.py`, `tests/test_maker_executor_engine_parity.py`).

**Inputs (all already produced by the backtester):** each fill (`_on_fill`, `backtester.py:1470`: venue, side, quantity, price, fee, maker or taker, timestamp), the funding rate records with their timestamps, the marks (book mids, `mark_inventory_venue`), the margin configuration. No order events, no signals.

**Core objects driven (same classes and constructors as `TradingSession`, `session.py:18-38`):** `Ledger` and `EconomicLedger` (`apply_fill`, opening balances), `FeeSchedule`, `FundingEngine`, the tiered margin evaluation (`RiskEngine.evaluate_margin`, `LiquidationEngine` read-only), `economics.reconcile` and `economics_gate`.

**Compared after every fill and at every settlement and mark:**
1. position per venue (BT `inventory.base` vs core contract balance, tolerance 1e-9 base);
2. cash and fees (two fee columns: the BT's own fee, and the core `FeeSchedule` rate, which is where C1 shows);
3. funding per settlement: amount, mark source, position used (C4);
4. margin state and liquidation price: BT `margin_model._breaches` vs core `_tiered_margin_row`, same marks (A9);
5. equity at the common-timestamp valuation vs the core's per-venue closing valuation (A2), and the BT equity bridge vs `economics.reconcile` identity (A3);
6. economics gate verdict (A4), with the reasons listed.

**Guarantee and test.** Switch `ArbBacktestConfig.core_shadow` (default None). The shadow only reads. Test: byte-identical result JSON, trade log and event log with the switch on and off, for every Step 1 scenario and for a real slice. The shadow holds copies of values; it never hands a core object back to the backtester.

**Mutation checks (Step 2 of the brief):** inject a wrong fee, a dropped fill, a wrong funding sign; each must produce a classified difference. "A skipped risk check" cannot be injected in this scope: risk is not in the loop, so that check moves to 4B. Say so at Gate 2.

**One new source file:** `new_backtest/core_shadow.py`, because the backtester must not import the core elsewhere and `CLAUDE.md` says to ask first. Approval needed. Alternative with no new file: put the shadow into `selftest.py` as a test-only harness (it already exists for wiring checks), at the cost of not being usable from a normal run. I recommend the new file.

**Known limits.** Order-level behaviour (states, cancel races, ack loss, reservations, risk verdicts) is not compared by this shadow. Differences found are sorted BUG_BACKTEST / BUG_CORE / BY_DESIGN; C1, C4 and C8 are expected to show up on day one.

### 4B. Deferred hedge child in the core (plan step 4; separate proposal, separate approval)

**The gap.** `OMS.execute()` takes an `Opportunity` and builds exactly two children (`oms.py:2566-2610`) and dispatches both; `DispatchPolicy` has one member (`execution.py:41`). The arb strategy's maker-first flow needs: send the maker child only; on each maker fill (partial included) create and send a hedge child for the filled quantity; never hedge more than has filled; one live hedge child per intent; block on unknown; unwind on failure.

**Design from the backtester's flow** (`backtester.py:1470-1545` `_on_fill`, `execution/layer.py`, `execution/intent.py`), expressed in core terms:
1. A new dispatch policy `FILL_TRIGGERED` next to `DETERMINISTIC_SEQUENTIAL`. The default stays unchanged, so every existing core test and result is untouched.
2. `execute()` under `FILL_TRIGGERED` creates the **maker child** at signal time and registers the parent plan with a **declared hedge leg** (venue, side, price cap, maximum quantity = the maker size) that has no `ChildOrder` yet. The hedge reservation is made atomically with the maker (EMS03): the whole package (both legs, worst case) is reserved up front, so a later hedge can never be refused for lack of room.
3. A new OMS method (name for the design stage) called when a maker fill is booked in `ingest_order_update`: creates the hedge `ChildOrder` for `filled - already_hedged`, persists it (`CHILD_DISPATCH_STARTED`, OMS03 protocol unchanged), then routes it.
4. **Revalidation at dispatch (EMS01, Q4):** before the hedge child is routed, re-check book freshness and tradability, the price cap (worst price), and the remaining limits; if the check fails the hedge is still sent when it is risk-reducing (it is: it closes exposure just taken) but with the cap rule, and the failure is recorded. This is the one place the core has no existing check, so it is new logic, not a port.
5. One live hedge child per intent and the unknown-intent block map onto the existing plan state machine and `ParentPlan.children` rules; the core's query-before-replace rule (OMS03) applies to this path; whether it removes the ghost over-hedge defect has to be shown by Step 1 scenario 5, not assumed.
6. Partial maker fills accumulate; a hedge for fill k may be in flight when fill k+1 arrives: rule needed (queue the second hedge until the first is resolved, or net). The backtester's current rule must be read and written down before design is final.

**What it does not do:** it does not add queue fills or the maker simulator (plan step 5, `QueueAwareVenueAdapter`), and it does not move the strategy.

**Tests first:** the 12 Step 1 scenarios with hand-calculated answers, run on the existing backtester, become the acceptance spec; the core change is accepted when the same scripts, fed through `TradingSession` with a basic adapter, give the same orders, fills, position and cash. Several scenarios (lost-ack ghost, restart with an open position) need the core's query-before-replace and persistence paths, which is why they are the useful ones.

**Risks:** (a) changing `execute()` or `ingest_order_update` touches the OMS02 reducer ported on 2026-11-01, so the full suite and the parity tests must stay green with `DETERMINISTIC_SEQUENTIAL`; (b) the core must commit separately on `consolidate-new-backtest` (CLAUDE.md two-repo rule), each change with owner approval; (c) the pre-trade check needs the instrument master and Decimal prices, so off-tick or sub-minimum backtester orders will be refused (C7 showing up).
